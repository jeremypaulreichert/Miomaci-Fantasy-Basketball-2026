# Independent prospective lessons for version 2

## Phase 1 boundary

Written before opening any repository material dated October20 or later, other
than this new report. Sources: my October19 pre-draft review/recheck, the recheck
brief, the October19 forecast-attempt amendment and counted-read/limits review.
I have not read subsequent code, results, amendments, seals or journals. The user
prompt itself reveals completion, multiple continuations and several amendment
categories; therefore this is **not genuinely outcome-blind preregistration**.
I exclude that supplied outcome narrative from the recommendations below and
anchor them to risks already documented at the recheck. Preserve this report
without retrospective editing after Phase2 starts.

## Biggest risks known at the recheck

1. **A successful draft could require changing the experiment while observing it.**
   Exact profile limits were already adapted after pilot failures. Validating a
   larger allowance does not establish that it will suffice for13 evolving turns.
   Unplanned per-seat rescue would confound model identity, harness behavior,
   available time and intervention intensity.
2. **Terminal interruptions were ordinary operational possibilities.** Truncation,
   unlimited-provider output, timeouts, exhausted correction pools and unknown
   dispatch could stop the run. Renewing authorization did not reset a pick's
   deadline or increase its budget. The read-feedback repair reduced a particular
   failure class, not all terminal states.
3. **Opening-board and synthetic successes were weak qualification for a complete
   live trajectory.** An all-Jokic consistency probe had little discrimination;
   synthetic130 demonstrated mechanics, not actual late-board strategy or latency.
   The pilot prompt also differed from counted wording.
4. **Evidence integrity could be confused with scientific validity.** Preserving
   every change makes a changing experiment auditable; it does not turn it into
   one fixed pre-registered condition. Local hashes do not establish public timing.
5. **Budget/time assumptions relied on averages.** Reported4.25 calls and$6 retained
   per pick left$1,000 vulnerable to slow tails, extra calls and unknown-usage
   ceilings. Vendor output behavior could violate the claimed per-call envelope.
6. **One shared draft could not establish company superiority.** Seat, available
   players, season luck, information timing and differing compute were entangled.
   Shared/solo scoring cutoffs and forecast retry selection needed explicit rules.

## Ranked version2 recommendations

### P1 / Rank1 — Freeze a complete incident and fallback state machine

Define before paid qualification which events are retried, corrected, deferred,
forfeited or fatal; exact limits; result eligibility; duplicate-charge accounting;
and whether a policy applies to every seat. Treat recognized fetch failure as a
tool outcome, with consumed allowance and negative evidence. Preserve hard stops
for corrupted identity/accounting and unknown execution until reconciled.

Separate provider reliability from basketball choice quality. A declared transport
failure is not evidence of poor sporting judgment. If a seat cannot pick within
the frozen recovery envelope, choose an explicit prospective fallback:

- **Clean primary experiment:** mark the opportunity forfeited, preserve the empty
  roster slot and define lineup feasibility/scoring and analysis denominators in
  advance. No organizer player substitution. If incomplete rosters are incompatible
  with the chosen estimand, declare the primary run incomplete and retain it.
- **Optional continuity arm:** use a separately sealed, AI-authored ranked fallback
  list and deterministic first-eligible execution. Label the selection automated
  from an AI's prior preference, not a new live AI decision. This is a different
  treatment, needs owner approval, and must not silently enter the live-choice
  primary analysis. Never invent a fallback list after seeing the failure.

Predeclare any budget top-up/authorization renewal without changing treatments.
Do not equate resumable authorization with arbitrary unpause. Keep a frozen
primary run and a separately labeled operational recovery study if broader rescue
is desired. Completion should not force retrospective rule invention.

### P1 / Rank2 — Qualify the full decision lifecycle, not one opening choice

Use the **exact counted prompt, schema, settings and tools** in qualification.
Maintain a small task bank: opening choice, constrained mid-round roster,
scarce-position late board, several owned/locked players, missing statistics,
failed page, exhausted tools and a deadline near expiry. Test legal choice and
factual support separately from agreement with expert ranking.

Run offline fault-injection through the real runtime and retain the full130 fixture
journal plus process exit and zero-send cold restart. Then run a bounded live
rehearsal spanning these contexts for every admitted seat, with repeated trials
where failures are variable. Declare admission gates before results and retain
exclusions. Rehearsal answers should not supply the counted season's entire draft
trajectory or silently train only selected seats via persistent context.

### P1 / Rank3 — Freeze per-seat limits and adequate time, with tail evidence

Equal numeric token caps are not equal effort. Pick either a capability-oriented
or cost-matched experiment and state it. Establish provider-supported reasoning,
output and context semantics; characterize output saturation and runtime tails
using representative tasks. Keep request ceiling, output validation, response-body
cap and reservation consistent. An empirically generous allowance is not an
enforceable vendor guarantee, particularly for xAI's reported unlimited output.

Freeze a per-seat schedule of limits before counted outcomes, including any
bounded escalation ladder available under objective triggers to all applicable
seats. Distinguish scheduled budget from actual usage. Include a usable final-answer
allowance where supported, rather than allowing all capacity to disappear into
reasoning. Do not claim private thinking is fully observable.

Use distinct per-request timeout, per-opportunity active-work budget, overall run
horizon and human-recovery grace period. Prespecify treatment of network versus
operator waiting. A genuine unknown dispatch must remain financially retained;
any resend requires a result-selection rule established before a second answer
exists. For a preseason study, complete before a fixed information cutoff or
classify the delayed arm separately; excluding early games from scoring does
not remove information observed during them.

### P1 / Rank4 — Seal source, analysis, inputs and amendments before requests

One manifest should identify rules, packet, exact profile/settings, seats/order,
baseline, qualification evidence, forecast-attempt selection, study outcomes,
fallback/recovery policy, limits and source snapshot. Independently timestamp it
before the first counted request if claiming public precommitment. Include the
original failed forecasts and explicit unknowns, not only successful re-asks.

Journal raw request/response identities, board and roster state, every tool result,
timing, usage completeness and interventions. Archive unsupported/unknown outcomes
without converting them to zero. Export must verify sealed bytes and distinguish
scheduled/reached/settled/failed/forfeited. Preserve amendments with actual timing
and authorizing evidence; never backdate them. A manual record is preferable to
an elaborate system that cannot reliably establish these basics.

### P2 / Rank5 — Improve prompt and board-state design without introducing coaching

Named aligned columns, explicit missing values, current legal IDs, all settled
opponent selections and the manager's roster/remaining positions should be
machine-checkable and consistent. Randomize packet row order once per replicate,
independently from seat order, and store the exact permutation. Validate all native
serializers for semantic parity. Replace profile-ID-dependent schema switches with
explicit versioned capabilities in a later tested release.

Keep operational rules in the manager prompt and scoring/grading criteria in the
observer. Distinguish invalid output from a valid, unconventional strategy. Do not
tell only a struggling seat which player to pick or provide basketball guidance
in an error message. If persistent research memory is included, give every seat
the same memory interface and freeze its retention/size rules.

### P2 / Rank6 — Repeat controlled draws and separate research questions

Treat the draft as an interacting multi-agent trajectory, not130 independent
observations. Run several independently seeded drafts or a balanced rotation of
seats, preserving comparable information cutoffs. Report uncertainty at the
replicate level; do not promise adequate statistical power from four draws alone.
Add a static, same-board repeated-choice benchmark for individual legal-choice,
fact use, research behavior and cost/reliability comparisons.

Keep no-competition solo rosters separate. Compare shared and solo under the same
eligible-game window and declare knowledge/memory separation. Forecast quality,
league winner and operational reliability are distinct outcomes. A calibration
score on each model's own selected25 is conditional, not a whole-pool common test.

### P2 / Rank7 — Budget from the complete policy and measured tails

Retain the per-dispatch ceiling gate and separate estimated charges, invoices and
unresolved exposure. Budget every model/tool/recovery call, not just the typical
three-call research sequence. Derive a base envelope plus a declared incident
reserve and prospective stop/top-up policy. Explain that a$1,250 buffer is still
not a guarantee when the permitted ungated policy is several thousand dollars.

Use offline recorded fixtures for plumbing regressions and a small real-provider
matrix for compatibility. Reuse unchanged verified evidence. Reserve large models
and repeated calls for the scientific question rather than review churn. Track
human operating time as well as tokens and dollars.

### P3 / Rank8 — Make conclusions and operations easy to audit

Publish a compact methods card, exact cohort, primary versus recovery results,
all failures/exclusions, retained costs and a minimal reproducible example.
Require a source snapshot including untracked implementation, not only a Git
bundle. Have an independent reviewer inspect the final frozen delta. Do not make
every presentation improvement a launch gate, or describe a hobby experiment as
a definitive ranking of AI companies. Its historical operational record can be
valuable even when its primary comparative design is inconclusive.

## Prospective priority

The highest-value change is an explicit, pre-tested failure/fallback protocol.
It protects completion **and** interpretability. More output tokens, longer
windows and more reviews alone cannot substitute for that protocol.
